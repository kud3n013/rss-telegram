Secretary of State Marco Rubio [dropped the hammer](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesman/2026/10/sanctioning-cuban-state-owned-banco-internacional-de-comercio-s-a-bicsa/) on Communist Cuba’s banking system Thursday, sanctioning a state-owned bank he says has been servicing a blacklisted Iranian lender for years.

Banco Internacional de Comercio S.A., known as BICSA, has provided services to Iran’s Karafarin Bank since at least 2022, according to the State Department. Treasury sanctioned Karafarin in 2020 as part of a crackdown on Iran’s financial sector.

“For too long, Communist Cuba has been allowed to operate as an outpost for malign actors and terror groups,” Rubio declared, singling out Iran as “the world’s number one state sponsor of terrorism.”

“The Trump Administration will not tolerate a rogue state hosting hostile foreign military, intelligence and terror operations less than a hundred miles from the American homeland,” he warned.

The move came alongside a Treasury action targeting Iranian financial actors. Rubio vowed to keep hunting down the Cuban regime’s financial and security ties to Iran and other “dangerous and hostile” actors.

The sanctions block any BICSA assets in the U.S. or in the possession or control of U.S. persons. U.S. persons were already generally prohibited from doing business with the bank without authorization. The action also put foreign banks on notice: certain dealings with sanctioned parties could expose them to sanctions. The executive order authorizes penalties against foreign persons operating in Cuba’s financial, energy, defense, mining, or security sectors.

Karafarin sits on Treasury’s [Specially Designated Nationals list](https://test.opensanctions.org/entities/NK-WEUs4ZrRRFHHnJQiZGpvSv), restricting its access to the global financial system. That makes a lifeline from Havana all the more valuable.

The Cuba–Iran romance goes back to the 1979 Islamic Revolution, built on a shared hatred of Washington rather than trade.

Fidel Castro [visited Tehran](https://diariodecuba.com/cuba/1773089006_65766.html) in 2001 and defended Iran’s nuclear program. In 2006, Cuba was one of only three countries, along with Syria and Venezuela, to vote against referring Iran’s nuclear dossier to the U.N. Security Council.

A July State Department [report](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/us/2026/07/20/cuba-us-state-department-report/) branded Iran one of Cuba’s main strategic partners. It accused Havana of providing intelligence, political and strategic support to Tehran and its terror proxies.

When the U.S. and Israel went to war with Iran in February, President Miguel Díaz-Canel’s government [condemned the strikes](https://www.breitbart.com/latin-america/2026/03/06/cuban-communists-honor-khamenei-reaffirm-support-to-iran-regime/amp/) as a violation of international law. After Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei was killed, Díaz-Canel signed a condolence book, and Cuba pledged its “unwavering support” to Tehran.

Thursday’s action is the latest blow in a relentless pressure campaign. It comes six weeks after Washington [blacklisted](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2627366/us-imposes-new-curbs-on-cuba) another state-owned lender, Banco Exterior de Cuba.

The [sanctions](https://www.cubanet.org/?p=449686) were issued under a May 1 executive order authorizing sweeping penalties on the island. In June, Rubio used that order to sanction Díaz-Canel himself, along with his wife and stepson. He also hit Raúl Castro’s son Alejandro Castro Espín, a [key figure in Cuba’s intelligence apparatus](https://web5.jamaica-gleaner.com/article/world-news/20260604/us-sanctions-cuban-president-miguel-diaz-canel-latest-move-pressure), and Cuba’s Ministry of the Revolutionary Armed Forces, which controls huge chunks of the economy.

Rubio, the son of Cuban exiles, has pushed back on reports of a softer approach. In March he [blasted](https://www.dailywire.com/news/charlatans-and-liars-rubio-torches-the-gray-lady-over-cuba-report) a New York Times story claiming Washington was negotiating a leadership swap in Havana, accusing the media of relying on “charlatans & liars.”

The message from the State Department is clear: Cuba’s ties to Tehran come with a price.
