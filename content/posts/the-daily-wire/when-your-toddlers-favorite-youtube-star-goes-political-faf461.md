Ms. Rachel never had a place in our home, but I know plenty of parents who feel differently.

The pink shirt, the childish headband, the overalls — it always felt too infantile, even for me, a mom with very young kids when the videos exploded onto the scene. We were stuck at home during a pandemic like everyone else. Like most other families, we explored YouTube for educational content to supplement the abysmal public school education our kids were getting via awkward Zoom calls. Thankfully, Ms. Rachel never made the cut.

But that’s not the case for everyone. And now parents whose kids fell in love with the songs, the vibe, and the mountains of merchandise are starting to figure out that the woman they welcomed into their homes wasn’t quite as innocent as she seemed.

By this point, it’s no secret that Ms. Rachel is not simply a children’s educator. She’s certainly no modern-day Mr. Rogers for the YouTube set. She has become an outspoken political activist, particularly on the Israel-Gaza conflict, immigration, and LGBT propaganda. And in January, her account was caught liking an Instagram comment that said, “Free America from the Jews.”

Rachel Accurso apologized and said the “like” was an accidental slip of the finger. She explained that she thought she was deleting the comment and had inadvertently selected the “like and hide” option. She also said she hates antisemitism. Whatever one makes of that explanation, the incident was another reminder that the woman in the pink shirt is considerably more politically engaged than her sing-song preschool videos might suggest.

**Putting on the pink headband**

Rachel Anne Griffin Accurso was born in Biddeford, Maine, in 1982 and raised in nearby Sanford. Before she became a YouTube celebrity, she was a teacher. She earned a master’s degree in music education from New York University in 2016 and later earned a second master’s degree in early childhood education. She met her husband, Broadway composer and music director Aron Accurso, in New York, and the two eventually became creative partners as well as husband and wife.

Their son’s speech delay would ultimately inspire the project that changed their lives.

In 2019, Rachel and Aron began producing “Songs for Littles,” a YouTube series built around nursery rhymes, songs, repetition, and speech-development techniques Accurso picked up from the pros who were working with her kid. She made videos that were deliberately interactive, with Accurso speaking directly to the camera, exaggerating facial expressions, repeating words, and giving children time to respond. Her quirky method caught on like wildfire.

Today, Ms. Rachel has more than 21 million YouTube subscribers and more than 17 billion views. One of her videos has been viewed more than 2 billion times, making her just as popular as some of the biggest music videos in the world. Her brand has expanded far beyond YouTube, too. Netflix now carries her show, Penguin Random House publishes her books, and there are branded Ms. Rachel toys available at major retailers such as Target and Amazon. She has legions of followers young and old on social media.

It’s clear that Ms. Rachel has become one of the most commercially significant figures in American children’s entertainment. And that matters because parents signed on for Ms. Rachel the sing-songy entertainer, not the anti-Israel activist.

Her appeal to kids was obvious. Parents may have found her irritating, but she had legitimate educational credentials and seemed to offer something more valuable than mindless screen time offering slop such as the frantically paced “CoComelon.” She taught toddlers words, letters, numbers, and social skills, all while presenting herself as a cheerful, bubbly adult who seemed genuinely to enjoy teaching. This was all before the activism became impossible to ignore.

**The ABCs of activism**

The first major shift came in 2024, when Accurso began using her enormous platform to speak out about the war in Gaza. She raised money for children affected by conflicts around the world but took a particular interest in Palestinian kids while repeating [disputed casualty claims](https://www.dailywire.com/news/why-i-dont-let-my-kids-watch-ms-rachel) and framing Israel as uniquely responsible for the suffering of children in Gaza, while omitting Hamas’ role in any of it.

She has repeatedly criticized Israel and has described its actions in Gaza as genocide. In September, she took her activism to another level when rapper Macklemore announced a [$1 million donation](https://variety.com/2026/music/news/ms-rachel-macklemore-donation-palestine-gaza-1236866020/) to organizations supporting Palestinians. Accurso said she would match the donation and challenged “every wealthy white celebrity” to do the same, calling it a “moral obligation.”

Then, on October 4, she publicly [criticized](https://www.instagram.com/p/DeFF_DTRaTf/?hl=en) celebrities who have remained silent about Gaza, questioning why people with enormous platforms would not risk their careers to speak out about the conflict. She has also criticized celebrities for failing to advocate for Palestinian children and has made Gaza a recurring subject of her social media presence.

Her political activism has not stopped with Israel.

Accurso has become an outspoken [advocate for LGBT causes](https://www.dailywire.com/news/the-political-transformation-of-ms-rachel-continues) and has publicly celebrated Pride. She has worked with and promoted LGBT-identifying performers, including “non-binary” collaborator Jules Hoffman, and has expressed interest in bringing controversial trans-identifying influencer Dylan Mulvaney into her children’s programming.

She has also increasingly spoken out [against immigration enforcement](https://www.dailywire.com/news/the-political-transformation-of-ms-rachel-continues), including participating in protests outside an ICE detention facility and discussing the treatment of children in immigration detention. Late last year, she [joined the inauguration committee](https://www.dailywire.com/episode/the-collab-toddlers-don-t-need-ms-rachel-x-mamdami) for New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani, an unmistakable leftist, and appeared with him at a preschool event.

She’s also putting money behind these causes. According to federal campaign finance records reviewed by [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/media/ms-rachel-builds-sprawling-media-empire-shelling-thousands-progressive-candidates), Accurso has donated approximately $22,500 to Democratic and progressive candidates since 2024. The recipients have included political committees associated with Kamala Harris, as well as progressive candidates including Abdul El-Sayed and others who are blatantly anti-Israel and anti-Trump.

**Too late to turn off?**

At some point, it becomes difficult to argue that Ms. Rachel is simply a children’s entertainer who happens to have political opinions. She has an enormous audience, a multimillion-dollar children’s-media empire, and a very clear set of political convictions that she’s not being quiet about. And it’s becoming obvious that the massive platform she built while singing to your children is the place she’s using to advocate for these causes.

This all leaves parents with a much more serious question than whether she’s annoying but worth it. Now it’s become a question of what to do when your kid’s favorite YouTube star promotes political opinions you find abhorrent.

Online commenters will claim the answer is usually simple: Turn it off. Speak out against her. Stop buying the merchandise. Stop giving the “woke” children’s entertainer your money. But parents know that parenting is rarely that simple.

The people giving that advice often seem to forget the part that comes before the boycott. They downplay the toddler who asks for Ms. Rachel every morning and plays the songs in the car while squeezing the overall-clad stuffy of her that she got for Christmas. These kids don’t know about Gaza, and anti-ICE protesters, and Mamdani, thank God. They just love Ms. Rachel.

That is what makes this particular controversy different from deciding that you no longer want to watch a movie star with wacky political opinions. Parents aren’t simply unfollowing a celebrity on Instagram because they understand she’s peddling a bunch of nonsense. They’re ripping away a part of their child’s world, and it can feel like a betrayal for everyone.

Maybe some parents will decide that Ms. Rachel’s educational value outweighs their disagreements with her politics. But as she becomes more aggressively political, many have felt that’s not working anymore.

Ms. Rachel built an empire by becoming a trusted adult in millions of American homes. Now she is using that enormous platform to talk about politics, war, immigration, and social issues that have nothing to do with teaching toddlers the ABCs.

The kids are still watching. What are they learning?
