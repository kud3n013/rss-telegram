**Eating together**

Ina Garten is my personal patron saint of dinner parties, using “good vanilla” and casually tossing around the phrase “how easy is that?” as I burn the cookies. But on a recent episode of her new pod “Happy Hour,” she hosted actor [Nathan Lane](https://youtu.be/1rnuZ7T-9OM?t=1368), who recalled an invitation to dine with legends of filmmaking Carl Reiner and Mel Brooks. Carl and Mel were famous for a 70-year friendship that at one point involved sharing a meal together in front of the TV (complete with little folding trays) at Carl’s Beverly Hills home every night.

When Carl died in 2020, Mel returned to his best friend’s home to eat dinner in front of the TV every night for a year. (I’m not crying; you’re crying.) Their daily routine was featured on an episode of “Comedians in Cars Getting Coffee” when Jerry Seinfeld interviewed Carl and brought takeout back for Carl and Mel from Nate ‘n Al’s (I’m undecided on the hot tongue sandwich Carl requested, but I’d take the chicken-in-a-pot that went to Mel).

I was so touched by the simple gesture of two friends sharing a meal. Mel is now 100 years old (Carl lived to be 98), but maybe those TV dinners had something to do with their longevity. Research shows that while eating in isolation can lead to sadness and depression, those who eat most meals with someone else (anyone you enjoy!) often live longer. I’ve heard [Arthur Brooks](https://www.facebook.com/watch/?v=958308343629896) say that eating with others strengthens emotional bonds and creates memories. According to him, “The point of food isn’t food. The point of food is life and love.”

**Poppin’ bottles**

With another high-profile heist hitting the headlines this week — this one involved [30,000 bottles](https://www.vice.com/en/article/italy-may-have-just-seen-its-biggest-wine-heist-ever-and-the-details-are-wild/) of Italian wine worth a cool 5 million euros — I’m once again a little envious of people with the talent for stealing things with a ton of flair. I apparently wasted my time playing soccer when I should have been developing James Bond’s life skills so I’d be on my mastermind friend’s speed dial to jump in on the greatest booze heist in Italian history. Can someone at least tell me where all this absconded vintage is allegedly being stored? I’ll bring the charcuterie.

In more wine news, one blend hopes to make your next trip through TSA totally worth the wait. Winemaker Jesse Katz collaborated with United to pop bottles that bring the most flavor at 35,000 feet — where our taste buds typically get a little meh. His solution? Actually blend the wine mid-flight to nail the perfect ratio of cab sauv, malbec, merlot, petit verdot, and cab franc for the latest offering from [Aperture Cellars](https://www.travelandleisure.com/united-airlines-creates-wine-for-flying-at-35000-feet-12167075). I’m pretending I’d ever be able to taste the difference between that and my ground-dwelling Bota Box pinot, but it’s about time air travel got an actual upgrade. Please pass those free peanuts with your pinky out.

**Footloose and ideology-free**

We all have our go-tos when everything in life feels like another lecture. Sports may be the last apolitical(ish) frontier. But locally, I also love my gym community where the laughter flows as we sweat for our lives, as well as a good movie (I just watched Guy Ritchie’s “The Ministry of Ungentlemanly Warfare” on Netflix. Five out of five stars — definitely recommend). Another great escape? Music. And leave it to a few heavy metal bros to keep it that way.
