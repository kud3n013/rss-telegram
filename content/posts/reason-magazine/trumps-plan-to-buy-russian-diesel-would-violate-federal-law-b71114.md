President Donald Trump says he has struck a deal with Russian President Vladimir Putin to import large amounts of diesel fuel into the United States—apparently hoping to lower fuel prices in the weeks before the midterm elections.

The exact terms of that agreement remain somewhat unclear. The White House has not said when the purchases will be made, what prices will be paid, or who will be paying (private companies or taxpayers), though the Treasury [announced](https://x.com/USTreasury/status/2108630834169385310) that it would be issuing a "temporary general license to allow the supply of Russian diesel to the global market." Reporting by *The New York Times* suggests that [investors with ties to the administration are most likely to profit](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/08/world/europe/trump-russia-oil-deal-lukoil-middle-east-investors.html). Because diesel prices are determined by a global market, it's also [far from certain](https://apnews.com/article/trump-russia-diesel-oil-prices-8428a31b39e20a3a3f1fa3f0e15b8f99) that the Russian gas would make much of a dent in American prices.

However, this much is clear: If Trump follows through with this "deal," he will have violated two federal laws recently passed by Congress—one of which he signed just weeks ago.

The more significant of those two laws is the [Ending Importation of Russian Oil Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/117th-congress/house-bill/6968/all-actions), which passed the House with a 413–9 vote and was unanimously approved by the Senate. President Joe Biden signed that bill into law on April 8, 2022. The law is an outright ban on Russian energy products, including diesel fuel.

The [law also lays out](https://www.congress.gov/bill/117th-congress/house-bill/6968) the process for lifting the ban on imports. The president must tell Congress that Russia has "reached an agreement to withdraw Russian forces and cease military hostilities in Ukraine" and has recognized "the right of the Ukrainian people to independently and freely choose their own government."

There is also a clear timeline for lifting the ban. Any termination of the ban requires a 90-day waiting period, during which time Congress has the opportunity to block the change.

Obviously, Russia has not withdrawn from Ukraine. Even if it had, the White House has not gone through the necessary process to notify Congress, nor waited the necessary 90 days.

The law does not allow a president to ignore it if fuel prices are high and an election is approaching. It does not allow a president to ignore it if he starts an illegal war in another part of the world that [causes fuel prices to spike](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/06/diesel-prices-inflation.html).

Trump's attempt to disregard that law has drawn a bipartisan rebuke from members of Congress. A group of seven senators—three Democrats and four Republicans—issued [a joint statement](https://www.foreign.senate.gov/press/dem/release/ranking-member-shaheen-and-6-bipartisan-senatorsstatement-on-legality-of-imports-of-russian-diesel-fuel) this week saying that Trump's deal "runs directly counter to" the 2022 law.

Sen. Thom Tillis (R–N.C.), one of the senators who joined that statement, [said on Sunday](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/10/11/tillis-trump-russian-diesel-deal-01115978) that there is "a legitimate case for saying the president's not following the law."

The second law at play is the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act, named for the late senator and passed earlier this year. Among other things, it authorizes the president to levy tariffs of up to 100 percent against countries that buy Russian crude oil and natural gas. The intent is clear: to prevent Russia from being able to finance its war against Ukraine by selling energy commodities onto the global market. Trump [signed it into law](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/russia/trump-signs-sweeping-new-russia-sanctions-bill-honor-lindsey-graham-rcna598675) last month.

Lawmakers on both sides of the aisle have also pointed to that law in the days since Trump announced his deal to buy Russian diesel.

"Now is the time to use those sanctions to squeeze Putin's war machine, not reward a dictator by putting more money in his hands while he continues targeting and killing Ukrainian civilians," Rep. Don Bacon (R–Neb.) [posted](https://x.com/RepDonBacon/status/2108665154976297148) on X this week. Rep. Steny Hoyer (D–Md.) [said](https://hoyer.house.gov/media/press-releases/hoyer-statement-trump-administration-decision-lift-sanctions-russian-diesel) it was "outrageous" for Trump to be buying energy from Russia after signing a bill that could be used to punish other countries for doing the same.

The White House could argue that, in this case, Trump is merely violating the spirit of the law rather than the letter of it. Even so, this is blatantly hypocritical.

The hypocrisy runs deep. The Trump administration has spent much of its second term trying to restrict trade and downplay the advice of economists who have warned that doing so would raise prices. Now, Trump is once again seeking imports as a means to ease high prices—as he has done with beef and other products. Perhaps the White House should apply this general principle more broadly, and in circumstances where doing so would not violate federal laws.

Some of the president's allies are now trying to downplay these violations of federal law. Conservative pundit Mollie Hemingway [says](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/mollie-hemingway-trumps-russian-oil-130000804.html) Trump is merely trying to "put the American people first" by addressing rising gas prices.

Sorry, but no. The president is not allowed to disregard federal laws because he believes it will lower gas prices in swing states.

You can, of course, criticize those laws for being misguided. Trade is generally good, and erecting barriers to the flow of goods tends to be costly. Trump could ask Congress to repeal these laws. He cannot deliberately ignore them.

If the "deal" with Russia comes to fruition, it will further erode what little power Congress retains within America's democratic system. If a president is not bound by the laws Congress has passed, then the legislature might as well not exist at all.
