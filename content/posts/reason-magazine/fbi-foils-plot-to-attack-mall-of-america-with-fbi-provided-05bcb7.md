Federal law enforcement officials in Minneapolis announced at a press conference last week that FBI agents arrested a Minnesota teenager plotting to commit a mass shooting at the Mall of America.

Sheikhdoon Abdullahi Mohamud, an 18-year-old American-born citizen of Somali descent, was arrested in a public park on October 6 by FBI agents immediately after he allegedly paid an undercover agent posing as a member of the Islamic terrorist group ISIS $600 for an AK-47 rifle and 200 rounds of ammunition. The arrest was the culmination of a two-year investigation into Mohamud, who allegedly planned to commit a mass shooting at the mega-mall on October 24.

According to the [criminal complaint](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.mnd.236610/gov.uscourts.mnd.236610.1.1_1.pdf) filed against Mohamud, he exchanged hundreds of messages with a confidential FBI informant expressing his desire to travel to Somalia to join ISIS, but lacking enough money to travel, he eventually settled for an alleged plot to attack the Mall of America because it was owned by Jews.

"I want to do this, I am very serious I don't care about anything in the world, I am 100% committed to the cause of dying for the sake of Allah only," Mohamud allegedly wrote in one text message, according to the complaint.

Christopher Dotson, a special agent running the FBI's Minneapolis field office [said](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/10/08/mall-of-america-alleged-attack-plot-details-chilling-messages-support-for-isis) at a press conference that Mohamud's messages "document the idolization of ideologically motivated violence, a callous disregard for the sanctity of human life, a blind hatred of people of other faiths, and a cold commitment to carrying out his vision of violence in our community."

As I explained in a 2022 *Reason* magazine feature, "[It's (Almost) Always the Feds](https://reason.com/2022/09/04/its-almost-always-the-feds/)," the FBI has a long history of using its sprawling network of paid informants to locate and bait would-be radicals in undercover sting operations.

The FBI's Countering Violent Extremism (CVE), created in 2011 to stop domestic recruitment by foreign terrorist organizations, has focused heavily on Somali immigrant communities in Minnesota and elsewhere because of Somalia's links to ISIS and al-Shabab, two Islamic terrorist groups.

An ISIS-affiliated account posted a video in 2015 [calling for an attack on the Mall of America](http://www.startribune.com/sept-21-2014-slick-videos-social-media-lure-minnesotans/275900041/). In 2016, a Somali man in the U.S. on a refugee visa stabbed 10 shoppers and employees at a St. Cloud mall. Last year, a Minneapolis man was [charged](https://apnews.com/article/minnesota-islamic-state-terrorism-new-orleans-attack-f71d0853b8326a211485f5f1b16ad1df) with attempting to join ISIS.

However, the sort of young men who usually get onto the FBI's extremist radar have grand and violent ambitions but limited means to carry them out. They tend to be unemployed or marginally employed, sometimes still living with their parents, and socially awkward.

In one text exchange, the confidential informant asked Mohamud how school was going.

"It's terrible man but I gotta keep doing it and if I fail its straight to just killin kuffar [a derogatory term for non-Muslims]," Mohamud responded, according to screenshots included in the criminal complaint . "And im already failing so if I don't pass in a yr ill probably save up for a gun."

The informant responded with the "grinning squinty face" emoji.

Mohamud continued to chat with confidential informants, praising terrorist attacks and sharing terrorist propaganda videos, according to the complaint. He didn't have a phone, so he had to use his sibling's computers and tablets to chat with informants. Last month, he recorded a video of himself pledging allegiance to ISIS on his little brother's iPad.

Despite persistent criticisms from civil liberties groups that the FBI is in many cases manufacturing terror plots to ensnare young men with limited to no ability to carry them out, the federal government has been enormously successful in securing convictions because of the high bar for defendants to prove entrapment.

The FBI's use of paid informants and these sting tactics have resulted in hundreds of convictions since 9/11, but it's also created long-term credibility problems for the bureau, especially when administrations have used those criminal cases to advance their political narratives.

For example, in a 2024 speech condemning political violence, former President Joe Biden [cited](https://reason.com/2024/07/15/biden-cites-the-farcical-fbi-assisted-plot-to-kidnap-gretchen-whitmer-as-an-example-of-political-violence/) the plot to kidnap Democratic Michigan Gov. Gretchen Whitmer. However, text messages and other records uncovered by defense attorneys during the case showed that the farcical plot was in large part concocted and encouraged by paid FBI informants and their bureau handlers.

"We have a saying in my office," one FBI special agent [told](https://www.woodtv.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/51/2021/12/Whitmer-kidnapping-trial-defense-motion.pdf#page=3) a confidential informant in the Whitmer case. "Don't let the facts get in the way of a good story."

The CVE program and related counterterrorism initiatives have also subjected tens of thousands of Somali refugees and other ethnic and religious minorities to heightened surveillance and infiltration by government informants, sowing distrust and fear, all based on a relatively small handful of incidents. *Reason*'s Eric Boehm [wrote](https://reason.com/2018/01/03/war-on-terror-minnesota-somali-immigrant/) in 2018 about how Minnesota's peaceful and entrepreneurial Somali refugee communities had become a domestic front in the war on terror, as well as microcosm of the national debate over immigration and national security.

None of this is to say the investigation into Mohamud was unwarranted, or that he wouldn't have been able to eventually get a gun absent the FBI's assistance. Nor is it a statement on his guilt or innocence, although he is as a matter of law innocent until proven guilty. It is a reminder to read carefully and wait for all the details to come out whenever the FBI announces it has thwarted another terrorist plot.
