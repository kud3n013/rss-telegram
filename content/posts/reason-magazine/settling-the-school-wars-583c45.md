**Durable changes:** This midterm season, activist groups have set their sights on amending state constitutions as a way of skirting never-ending culture war battles.

In Nebraska, voters are being asked to consider "enshrin[ing] in its constitution an existing law that prohibits transgender students from playing on girls' and women's sports teams," [reports](https://www.washingtonpost.com/education/2026/10/13/school-prayer-trans-athletes-other-education-issues-are-midterm-ballots/) *The Washington Post.* "It also would require school teams to be designated as male, female or co-ed." The campaign behind it, [Fairness for Girls](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/09/02/nebraska-joins-arizona-colorado-and-washington-as-first-states-to-vote-on-sex-based-eligibility-for-school-sports-ballot-measures-in-2026/), turned in 211,000 signatures in June, and its $3.1 million in cash contributions as of July 31 came entirely from a single nonprofit, Restore the Good Life Inc.

Voters in [Washington](https://www2.sos.wa.gov/_assets/elections/initiatives/finaltext_3276.pdf), [Colorado](https://www.sos.state.co.us/pubs/newsRoom/pressReleases/2026/PR20260316Ballot109.html), and [Arizona](https://azsos.gov/sites/default/files/docs/2026-Ballot-Language-English-2026-0819.pdf) will see similar measures on their ballots related to transgender athletes and what schools should allow—though these would change state law rather than constitutions. Arizona's was put on the ballot by the Republican Legislature, while Colorado's and Washington's came from citizen petitions. Transgender students who say they are girls are already barred from girls' school teams under Arizona state law; the state's [Proposition 318](https://www.newsfromthestates.com/article/top-republicans-embrace-anti-trans-measure-wedge-november-races) would now ban student athletes from using restrooms, locker rooms, and showers that don't match an athlete's sex at birth, plus extend the rules to private schools and club sports.

Meanwhile in Alabama, voters will be asked whether the state's constitution should be altered to require public schools to allow prayer—led by students—at the start of the day. The same amendment would also put the daily Pledge of Allegiance, which state law already requires, into the constitution. Students couldn't be forced to take part in the prayer and would be able to opt out of the pledge.

Interestingly, Alabama has been here before. In [*Wallace v. Jaffree*](https://www.oyez.org/cases/1984/83-812) (1985), the Supreme Court struck down an Alabama statute authorizing a moment of silence "for meditation or voluntary prayer." It's not clear why they're trying basically the same thing all over again. ("What is the oversight power of the school in something like this?" Steven P. Brown, a professor of political science at Auburn University, [asked](https://alabamareflector.com/2026/10/12/alabama-voters-to-consider-pledge-of-allegiance-national-anthem-school-prayer/) the *Alabama Reflector.* "If you have a kid who stands up and wants to give a prayer to Satan, there is no question the school is going to step in." It's a fair question!)

"This year has brought a total of 19 ballot initiatives related to K-12 and higher education, the most since the midterms in 2018, [according to a database](https://app.powerbi.com/view?r=eyJrIjoiYjEwNDI2NTctZDFkMy00ZGM4LWFkMTItNTcwYTdkZmMxMGIxIiwidCI6IjM4MmZiOGIwLTRkYzMtNDEwNy04MGJkLTM1OTViMjQzMmZhZSIsImMiOjZ9) maintained by the National Conference of State Legislatures," [reports](https://www.washingtonpost.com/education/2026/10/13/school-prayer-trans-athletes-other-education-issues-are-midterm-ballots/) *The Washington Post.*It seems like activists are growing tired of constantly relitigating culture-war issues, and instead hope to create a more durable consensus via amendments to state constitutions and voter-approved statutes that are harder for legislators to alter.

**Dissent:** House Speaker Mike Johnson (R–La.) has [sounded off](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/10/12/mike-johnson-says-execution-video-could-be-recruiting-tool-terrorists/) about Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's idea of livestreaming the execution of Nidal Malik Hasan, the former Army major who killed 13 people in Fort Hood, Texas, in 2009.

Johnson says the video of the execution by firing squad could become "a recruiting tool for future terrorists," according to Johnson. "We've got to think through the policy implications of all that," Johnson [told](https://www.cbsnews.com/video/house-speaker-mike-johnson-voices-opposition-to-livestreamed-execution-planned-by-hegseth/) CBS News on Monday.

"I wouldn't be watching it if it was aired," Johnson said. He added that someone should tell the White House "what some of my colleagues think about that, and not everybody's in favor of the idea." Reps. Austin Scott of Georgia, Tom Barrett of Michigan, and Rob Bresnahan of Pennsylvania have also voiced their opposition to the firing squad/livestream combo execution.

On Friday, Vice President J.D. Vance [referred](https://apnews.com/article/fort-hood-execution-hegseth-vance-e806a305d9c6460aa20b14e40047680e) to the livestream, saying, "I don't know that that's actually going to happen" and said that, regardless, he wouldn't be watching it.

"Public executions are what oppressive dictatorships like Iran and Afghanistan do," [said](https://apnews.com/article/fort-hood-execution-hegseth-vance-e806a305d9c6460aa20b14e40047680e) Sen. Thom Tillis (R–N.C.) [this past Thursday](https://thehill.com/homenews/senate/6138373-thom-tillis-pentagon-live-firing-squad-execution-nidal-hasal-criticism/). "It should not be happening in America."
