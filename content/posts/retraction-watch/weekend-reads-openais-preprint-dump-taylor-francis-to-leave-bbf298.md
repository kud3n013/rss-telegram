If your week flew by — we know ours did — catch up here with what you might have missed.

The week at Retraction Watch featured:

- Editor-in-chief resigns amid probes of alleged [citation manipulation, data errors](https://retractionwatch.com/?p=136141)
- Wildlife journal issues corrections for [missing ethics statements](https://retractionwatch.com/?p=136163)
- A paper suggested a paleontologist’s fossil was a forgery. [He wants it retracted](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/10/08/mosasaur-xenodens-calminechari-fossil-forgery-authenticity-paleontology/)
- OpenAI [withdraws three preprints](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/10/08/openai-withdraws-preprints-722-manuscripts-unsolved-math-problems/) 1 day after releasing 722 manuscripts on unsolved math problems.
- ‘Cerebral torment’: IOP retracts nearly 100 conference papers [for tortured phrases](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/10/09/iop-publishing-conference-proceedings-tortured-phrases/)

In case you missed [the news](https://retractionwatch.com/2025/12/26/retraction-watch-hijacked-journal-checker-now-has-400-entries/), the [Hijacked Journal Checker](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1ak985WGOgGbJRJbZFanoktAN_UFeExpE/edit?gid=5255084#gid=5255084) now has more than 450 entries. The [Retraction Watch Database](https://gitlab.com/crossref/retraction-watch-data) has over 67,000 retractions. Our [list of COVID-19 retractions](https://retractionwatch.com/retracted-coronavirus-covid-19-papers/) is approaching 700, and our [mass resignations list](https://retractionwatch.com/the-retraction-watch-mass-resignations-list/) has more than 50 entries. We keep tabs on all this and more. If you value this work, please consider showing your support with a tax-deductible donation. Every dollar counts.

Here’s what was happening elsewhere (some of these items may be paywalled, metered access, or require free registration to read):

- “It’s been an open secret for many years that [Informa would consider selling Taylor & Francis](https://newsletter.journalology.com/p/separation-taylor-and-francis-to) if the price was right.”
- “arXiv’s [new](https://blog.arxiv.org/2026/10/01/updated-rate-limit-policy/) preprint submission cap [divides opinion](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/arxivs-new-preprint-submission-cap-divides-opinion).”
- India’s NIRF university rankings delayed as board considers “[debarring” repeat misconduct offenders](https://theprint.in/india/education/slew-of-retracted-research-papers-flagged-in-nirf-assessment-puts-academic-institutes-under-govt-lens/3062852/) after including retraction data in their analysis.
- The [Yannick Griep case](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/07/14/sage-retracts-eight-papers-by-former-radboud-rising-star-for-compromised-peer-review-process/) “reveals why cheating in science is so persistent, and [what can be done about it](https://www.volkskrant.nl/wetenschap/vijftien-jaar-na-de-affaire-stapel-is-er-weer-een-sjoemelwetenschapper-gepakt-ik-ben-niet-eerder-zo-n-grote-casus-tegengekomen~b5b63b81/).”
- “[Statistical Sleuthing: Can you spot a problem](https://www.normalcurves.com/statistical-sleuthing-can-you-spot-a-problem/)?”: A podcast featuring the recent [Dan Ariely retraction](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/09/03/procrastination-study-duke-dan-ariely-psychological-science-data-colada-tampering-retraction/).
- “Effect of research paper mills enough to start [affecting LLM query results](https://news.err.ee/1610155906/effect-of-research-paper-mills-enough-to-start-affecting-llm-query-results),” says researcher.
- Scientists don’t have a “religious calling that keeps them away from the [temptations of the real world](https://www.youtube.com/live/qy-rjpH8Uo4),” says Nick Brown.
- “The systemic breach of AI in academic publishing: Infrastructural failures and authorial misconduct across [seven major publishers](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/08989621.2026.2741356).”
- “Peer review isn’t free. [So who pays](https://www.researchinformation.info/analysis-opinion/peer-review-isnt-free-so-who-pays/)?”
- Don’t mandate AI prompt disclosure, argue [ethicists](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00146-026-03326-w). A composer who [drew criticism](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/08/05/a-composer-published-a-paper-on-theoretical-physics-his-lack-of-credentials-drew-criticism/) for publishing a paper on theoretical physics [responds](https://jmepb.bmj.com/content/2/3/e000370), and says “AI companies have [hijacked academic norms](https://blogs.bmj.com/medical-ethics/2026/10/05/ai-companies-have-hijacked-academic-norms/).”
- “The editors-in-chief of Science, Nature, Cell, and NEJM AI on what journals now require when AI does the analysis, [and who gets the credit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q04PAohvSYU).”
- “The [Limited Communication of Corrections](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/leap.2103) to Users of Scholarly Platforms.”
- “Publishers to [share 20 million research papers](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/publishers-share-20-million-research-papers-ai-start) with AI start-up.”
- “CDC Is Moving Forward With a Previously Shelved [Controversial Hepatitis B Vaccine Study](https://www.rollingstone.com/politics/politics-news/cdc-controversial-hepatitis-b-vaccine-study-1235637894/).” Arthur Caplan and Jonathan Moreno previously wrote “The CDC hepatitis B study is [unethical and must never be published](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/02/10/guest-post-cdc-hepatitis-b-study-ethics-must-never-be-published/).”
- “Should AI Have [a Role in Peer Review](https://www.the-scientist.com/should-ai-have-a-role-in-peer-review-75085)?” and “The [Conversation We’re Not Having](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3840586.3843746) about AI in Peer Review.”
- “Available upon reasonable request: the [authorship function that was already dead](https://jmepb.bmj.com/content/2/3/e000330).”
- “‘[Organized academic harassment](https://cen.acs.org/research-integrity/misconduct/organized-academic-harassment-hokkaido-university/104/web/2026/10)’ at Hokkaido University Chemistry Department.”
- “What can chemists learn from [how AI is shaking up mathematics](https://cen.acs.org/research-integrity/editorial-artificial-intelligence-chemists/104/web/2026/10)?”
- “The fast-track to publication: [bibliometric analysis of rapid service fees](https://zenodo.org/records/22656990) in scholarly publishing.”
- “Editors are facing an influx of AI-written pitches, including at science publications like mine. [What now](https://www.cjr.org/analysis/fake-caroline-williams-science-nature-magazine-ai-influx-pitches.php)?”
- Journal editors on how they are coping with “the impacts of generative artificial intelligence [on scholarly publications](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08989621.2026.2737718).”
- Researcher proposes preprint servers “[integrate a mandatory data sanitization checklist](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03177-x) into the submission pipeline” to prevent [leaking sensitive information](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02057-8).
- Authors “[reject the use of the term ‘scientist](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/genetics/articles/10.3389/fgene.2026.1954843/full)’” to refer to AI agents in scholarly publishing.
- “OpenAI Fires Researchers for Allegedly [Sharing Information with AI Safety Group](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/openai-parts-ways-with-researchers-who-allegedly-shared-confidential-information-aebac528).” The researchers “dispute misconduct claims, [warn of chilling effect](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/fired-openai-safety-researchers-dispute-misconduct-claims-warn-of-chilling-effect/)” in [open letter](https://mikitabalesni.com/letter/letter.pdf).
- “One small study in Iran [swayed meta-analyses in favour of antidepressant use in children](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02769-x), researchers say.”
- Former university president [added former school vice president to defamation lawsuit](https://www.dailywire.com/news/after-plagiarism-was-exposed-u-s-colleges-protected-culprits-punished-whistleblowers) following plagiarism accusations.
- “Every outcome in the U.S. News rankings seemed to be good news this year according to a wave of press releases that touted how well colleges performed—[even in cases where they slipped](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/governance/executive-leadership/2026/10/02/how-colleges-spin-rankings-outcomes).”
- “AI agents are not good at interpreting science. [What can be done](https://cen.acs.org/policy/publishing/ai-agents-interpreting-science/104/web/2026/10)?”
- “Ten Priorities for Scholarly Publishing [through 2027](https://www.researchinformation.info/analysis-opinion/opinion-ten-priorities-for-scholarly-publishing-through-2027/).”
- “How did Rosalind Franklin miss the helix in her iconic DNA image? [She didn’t](https://www.science.org/content/article/how-did-rosalind-franklin-miss-helix-her-iconic-dna-image-she-didn-t).”
